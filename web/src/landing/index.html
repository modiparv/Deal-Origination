Origination screening on filed registry data,
where every figure traces to a filing.

A UK lower-mid-market screening engine built on Companies House filings. No figure on any screen is computed by a language model; each one is a filed observation carrying its source document, tag, period end and filed date — and a one-click trace to the actual filing on the public register.

Open the screen results → Operations & coverage

Provenance, transitively

Click any figure and walk the chain: figure → inline-XBRL tag → source document (with its producing software and content hash) → filing date and registry transaction → the filing itself on find-and-company. Derived figures walk back through every input to its own source. A filed figure without a source document cannot be persisted.

Absence carries a cause

Most UK small companies file no public P&L. The engine never papers over a gap: every missing concept states its recorded reason — filed_without_concept, unparseable_format, parse_failed, not_reached, not_captured — as distinct, first-class states. A parse failure is reported as a system defect, never disguised as a data limitation.

Staleness shown, never hidden

Filed registry data is 9–21 months old by construction, and worse in the tail. Every figure shows its period end, filed date and age; profiles lead with the freshest machine-readable period. Reports state dated public evidence of absence — never the unevidenceable claim that a company is “not being marketed”.

What you will not find here: financial figures invented or computed by a language model, aggregator-sourced numbers, sample companies, or placeholder values. If a screen has no data, it says so.
